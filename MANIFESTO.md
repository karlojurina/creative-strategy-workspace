# The Manifesto

This is how we use this workspace to do creative strategy.

Not a list of ad tricks - the mindset and the method behind everything you do in here. Most of it comes from two places: Eugene Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising* (read it more than once) and years of doing this in the trenches. Read it before you start, come back to it whenever you lose the thread, and when a skill's output ever fights one of these, the manifesto wins.

## First, what creative strategy actually is

Picture your customer standing on one side of a gap. That is their current state - where they are right now, with whatever problem or frustration they carry. On the other side is where they want to be: their desired state, their ideal life. They want to cross, but they cannot do it on their own.

Your job is to build them a bridge, and that bridge is the product.

Creative strategy is the whole craft of understanding that person well enough to build the *right* bridge, and then communicating it so clearly that they actually cross. It lives at the intersection of four things: the customer, their desire, the product, and the market. Get all four right and the ad almost writes itself. Miss one and the bridge does not reach, no matter how good it looks.

Everything below is how you build that bridge, over and over, without guessing.

## How we work in here

### Preparation beats talent
The most prepared, most knowledgeable marketer makes the most money. Not the most creative one, not the most talented one. Two out of three times, a careful "mid" marketer beats a sloppy "great" one, because attention to detail and work ethic beat ego every time. Your one edge that no one can take from you is being more accurate and more knowledgeable than anyone you come up against. 

### You do the thinking. AI does the grunt work.
This workspace will never make you a marketer on its own. It hands you building blocks - research, claims, drafts - and does the tedious lifting so you can spend your energy where it counts. But the understanding has to live in your head, not the model's. Know your customer, your market, and your product cold. The second you let AI do the thinking for you, you become average, because everyone else has the same AI. Garbage in, garbage out. What you put in is the whole game. You have to be able to do every step by hand first. You cannot direct, or even recognize, work you have never produced yourself. Learn to research, write, and build the ad manually, and then AI becomes a multiplier - give it your expertise and you get expertise back, give it nothing and you get slop.

### Copy is assembled, not written
You are not writing an ad. You are building a city of desire for your customer to come live in. You assemble it from building blocks - claims, images, desires - and you keep rearranging them until it works. So the AI draft is never the finished ad. It is a starting point. You take it out of the AI chat interface, into a space where you can see the whole thing at once (we build ours in Milanote), and you assemble the real ad brief yourself - with your own hands, your own visual references, and fresh eyes. That distance from the AI interface is where the good stuff happens.

### Close the loop - on your ads and on your tools
Every ad teaches you something, win or lose. The marketers who never improve are the ones who never stop to ask *why* an ad worked or flopped and then feed that answer back in. So you close the loop on every ad: pull the learning, write it down, use it in the next one. And you run the same loop on this workspace itself - every session, look for one thing that could be sharper, and fix it. Ads get better, tools get better, you get better. That is the whole engine.

## How to be creative

### Creativity is connectivity
Forget "being creative." The word has no clear meaning and it scares people. Eugene Schwartz replaced it with a better one: connectivity. Being creative is just connecting two things that logically would not go together, until you connected them. A brand new connection. That is all an original ad idea ever is - two dots connected for the first time.

### It runs on two minds
Your conscious mind handles logic, daily tasks, and solving clear problems. Your unconscious mind is the one that actually connects the dots and hands you the idea. But it can only work with what you have fed it. So creativity is not a gift you are born with. It is the result of two things: great inputs, and giving your brain the space to connect them.

### Feed the machine good inputs
The dots are your inputs, and they come in two kinds. **Information inputs**: a deep understanding of the customer, the market, the competitors, the product. This is why research is never optional. And **visual inputs**: staying curious about ad and content formats, keeping your own swipe file of visual work you love. The more and better your dots, the better the connections your unconscious can make.

### Work focused, connect unfocused
Your focused mind can only hold a handful of words at once, and when it is focused it can only reach for the logical, obvious ones. The new connections live in the cloud of unfocused thought around them. That is why you cannot force an idea by staring at a blank page. You work in focused blocks, then you rest, and the idea shows up while you are in the shower, walking, doing something else entirely. When it does, write it down immediately, because you *will* forget it. And keep a few things going at once, so your unconscious can chew on one while your conscious mind works another.

## The craft

### Hunt claims
Research is claim-hunting. Go through everything front to back - reviews, comments, competitor ads, the product itself - and highlight every claim you find. Pull them all into one place. Then pick the strongest ones and build from those. Your pile of raw claims is the raw material for every ad you will ever make for that brand. (We call this the research document)

### Read the market before you build
Two lenses decide how you talk to anyone. **Market awareness**: how much does this person already know - are they unaware they even have the problem, or down to comparing your product against three others? **Market sophistication**: how many times has this market heard this claim before - are you the first, or is everyone already shouting it? You do not write a word until you know where the market sits, because the same product needs a completely different ad at each stage.

### Know exactly who you're building for
You cannot sell to someone you do not understand. Get to know your customer better than they know themselves - their reality, their fears, and the desires they would never say out loud. And who you are building for decides far more than your words: it shapes the format, the pacing, the music, the visuals, which features you pull forward, how blunt or how gentle you are. The same product sold to a 20-year-old gym buddy and to your grandmother is two completely different ads. Write copy that is YOUful - full of "yous," talking straight to that one person - and aim for the highest bar there is: they see it and think "this is literally me." Sometimes you earn that by spelling out the transformation; sometimes you earn it just by showing them already living their dream state, so they recognize themselves and want in. And know this: on today's platforms the creative itself is the targeting - make it unmistakably for your person, and the algorithm carries it to exactly them. 

### You channel desire, you do not create it
You cannot make someone want something they do not already want. Desire is already out there - mass desire, running through millions of people. Your job is to take that existing desire and channel it onto the product. Find what they already want, then show them the product is the way to get it. And not every desire is worth the same. Weigh three things: intensity (how badly they need it right now), staying power (does it keep coming back), and scope (how many people share it). A product can satisfy several desires, but only one is the dominant desire that scales it to real numbers. Find that one (you find it by testing a bunch of different ads), connect it to the product, and speak to it.

### You're not selling the product, you're selling the transformation
Every product is really two products. The physical product - features, specs, ingredients, the object itself. And the functional product - what it does for the person, the transformation, the feeling they walk away with. Almost every brand sells the first one. You sell the second. So stop asking "what does this product do?" and start asking "who does my customer become when they use it?" A high-RPM motor in the hair dryer is not the sell - twenty minutes back a person gets every morning is. Schwartz said it plainly: list everything the product does, match each performance to a desire people already have, then feature the single one that carries the most sales power.

### Respect the feed
Nobody is on social media to watch your ads. They came to escape, to feel something, to be entertained. Your ad is an interruption, and their brain is lazy - it runs on glucose, it hates burning fuel, and it filters out almost everything to save energy. So every ad comes down to one question: how hard does the brain have to work to get this? The easier it is to process, and the more it feels like a post instead of an ad, the more it slips through. Clarity always beats clever. Confuse them for a second and they are gone.

### See through the words
Words in an ad are like the windows in a store - the reader should look straight through them and see the product, not the writing. Copy should never call attention to itself or try to sound clever. Its only job is to create a picture in the customer's head so vivid they can feel it.

### The headline's only job is the next line
A headline is not supposed to sell, define the brand, or carry the whole ad on its back. Its one job is to be tantalizing enough that they read the next line. Then that line earns the one after it. Great copy is not paragraphs, it is a series of headlines, each one pulling the reader deeper.

### Claim plus mechanism
When a market has heard every claim a hundred times, the claim alone stops working, and the mechanism carries the ad. Be melodramatic and polarizing when you need to be, paint heaven and hell, but always honest and always fair.

## The through-line

Every one of these rolls up to the same thing: the person who is more prepared, who does the real work themselves, and who actually understands the human on the other end - wins. Not the one with the best AI, the flashiest ad, or the most natural talent. That is how you beat people more gifted than you, and it is exactly what this workspace is built to make you.
