# Metaglow - Turning a Losing Ad Into a Winning Ad

**The loop this ad proves:** almost-winner dies -> real learnings as a team -> diagnose the script's gaps -> rewrite with more education -> stronger winner in a new concept.

| | |
|---|---|
| Product | Metaglow Collagen - marine collagen that naturally boosts GLP-1 via a trademarked ingredient (the only marine collagen that contains it) |
| Avatar | Postpartum women struggling to lose the baby weight |
| Ad 1 (the almost-winner) | Voiceover video; top spender for ~7 days, then died |
| Ad 2 (the winner) | Text-on-screen, no voiceover, slower, 2x longer - and much more persuasive |
| The play | Villain hormone (prolactin) -> new-hope mechanism (GLP-1) -> the only product with the ingredient |

## Ad 1: what worked and what didn't

The first ad opened with *"After birth, women have ten times the normal levels of the fat-storing hormone known as prolactin"* and showed real potential before dropping off. The team processed learnings properly: a learnings call with a note-taker, the transcript run through a learnings prompt (context: how we run ads, CBO campaigns, what the call is for), the summary saved for the next script.

**Why it showed potential:**
- **New mechanism in the hook.** "Fat-storing hormone" is new information for this avatar - it creates curiosity.
- **It met them where they are.** These women count calories, work out, do everything right, still hold the weight - and blame themselves. The ad said: it is not your willpower, it is your hormones. **Shifting blame from personal failure to biological reality** is the emotional core of the whole angle.
- **Scientific authority without being technical.** "Prolactin," "10x normal levels" - credible, still readable.

**Why it died - the diagnosis (this is the masterclass part):**
- **The product came in too early.** One line of education, then "that's where Metaglow comes in." The viewer was not yet convinced the hormone story was real - the belief wasn't built, so the product intro felt like a disconnect and the less-educated viewers dropped.
- **The team's own note: "the script was almost a bit shallow, with no narrative around it."** Read your own script line by line in the customer's shoes and ask at every line: are they ready for the next one yet?
- **The unique mechanism never showed up.** The trademarked ingredient - the thing only this product has - wasn't even mentioned. The bridge from "hormones are the problem" to "this specific product" had nothing to stand on.

## The rewrite process

1. **Write the diagnosis in your own words first.** Before prompting anything, the learnings were re-typed as intent: be more persuasive about the hormone story, keep it in the customer's language, introduce the ingredient as the mechanism BEFORE the product, build a narrative.
2. **Make the AI research before it writes.** Claude was sent to Reddit first: what are postpartum moms saying about their weight-loss journey, how do they talk, how do they feel. This warms the model up, and it picks up the avatar's real language and niche terms (that is where "new mom stress keeps cortisol high" came from). Ask for the source links and read the threads yourself.
3. **Then the script prompt, loaded with everything:** old script, learnings summary, the diagnosis, the mechanism to feature, the execution format.
4. **Take it OUT of the chat.** The draft goes into the brief document and gets read line by line, in the viewer's shoes: is the problem explained well enough? Is the bridge to the product earned? Feedback goes back to Claude in specific terms ("this part introduces the product too early because...") until the structure holds. You assemble the final script - the AI never ships it.

## Ad 2: what changed

- **Hook (credit: Kat):** *"Here's why gaining weight postpartum is normal."* The entire market screams "lose 20 pounds in 4 weeks." This says the opposite - it agrees with her, tells her nothing is wrong with her, and she HAS to know why. Anti-hype as pattern interrupt.
- **Education before product.** Prolactin explained simply ("your body thinks it needs reserves for breastfeeding"), the problem made bigger ("this can last months, even years"), THEN the science turn ("scientists discovered boosting GLP-1 helps your body burn stored fat"), THEN the ingredient ("the only collagen supplement that contains it"), and only then the product - a minute twenty in, versus halfway in ad 1.
- **Relatable translation of the mechanism:** "Prolactin is screaming EAT EVERYTHING. GLP-1 helps you say no to cravings." Everyone understands that.
- **Text-on-screen, no voiceover, deliberately slow.** The theory on why text-on-screen keeps winning: the viewer reads the ad with their INNER voice - they are not being sold to, they are selling the product to themselves.
- **Honest CTA:** "Try it 60 days risk-free - because real results take time." No over-promising. For a sensitive avatar (moms), radical honesty beat every high-energy promise in the market.

## What to steal

- **An almost-winner is a message from the market.** Seven days of spend then death = the angle works, the persuasion doesn't. Learn, don't shrug.
- **The #1 script killer is introducing the product before belief is built.** Educate until the product feels inevitable.
- **Feature your unique mechanism.** If only your product has it, the bridge is free - use it.
- **Run every script through Hemingway** (hemingwayapp.com): grade 6 readability or below, or it is too complicated and people will scroll.
- **Match the concept to the avatar's sensitivity.** Moms don't want negativity or hype - agree with them, remove the guilt, educate gently.
