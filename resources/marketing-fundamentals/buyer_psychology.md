# Buyer Psychology - Why People Buy Things

The answer is one word: **desire.** A strong feeling of wanting something - the thing behind every purchase, every market, every winning ad. Master three fundamentals and you can make winning ads for anything: people buy from desire they already have; you find desires by understanding the avatar deeply; you sell by connecting the product to those desires.

(The deep source for all of this is Breakthrough Advertising - "pretty much the bible of this game," a book you actually have to STUDY, not just read. The [summary](../breakthrough-advertising/breakthrough_advertising_summary.md) is the working reference. No flashy tactics here - fundamentals, because fundamentals are timeless.)

## Law #1: You channel desire. You never create it.

**"We don't create the desire, and we never try to create the desire. We simply just channel it towards our product."** If you try to make people want things they don't already want, you will fail - and no ad budget saves you.

Think of desire as a natural force, like sun or wind - **a river flowing through a market. Your job as a marketer is to dig a small channel to direct some of that desire toward your product.** When enough people privately want the same thing, a market is born. The river is already flowing; you don't have to push the water.

Channeling takes three things:
1. **Knowledge of the desire** - what the market already wants.
2. **Understanding of your product** - what it can actually deliver.
3. **The ad that connects the two.**

## Not all desires are equal

Every product touches several desires; grade them before you pick:

| Dimension | The question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Intensity** | How badly do they need it right now? | Migraine vs mild headache |
| **Staying power** | Does it keep coming back? | Staying young vs one pimple |
| **Scope** | How many people share it? | Get in shape vs run an ultramarathon |

The job: **find the strongest, most dominant desire behind the product.** Every product can do multiple things, but only ONE dominant desire scales it to big numbers. You find it by testing different ads, not by guessing in a document.

## Law #2: Every product is two products

- **The physical product** - features, specs, ingredients, the object itself.
- **The functional product** - what it does for the person: the transformation, the feeling.

Nobody buys the first one. **"Nobody cares about the product. They only care about what it does for them."** A high-RPM motor means nothing; *you save 20 minutes every morning* means everything. Whisper-quiet means nothing; *you can style your hair while your family is sleeping* is the sell.

**You're not selling products - you're selling a transformation.** The customer stands in their current state; across the gap is their dream state; your product is simply the bridge between the two. So stop asking "what does this product do?" and start asking **"who does my customer become when they use it?"** Schwartz's method, verbatim from the book: list the product's performances, group them against the mass desires each one satisfies, and "feature the one performance that will harness the greatest sales power onto your product at a particular time."

Most ads fail here for a boring reason: all brands are just copying each other, and no one's really doing deep research.

## Law #3: Know them until the ad feels personal

The effect every great ad produces - "how did they know?" - is never magic. It's a marketer who understood the customer so deeply the ad feels made for them specifically. The bar: they see it and say **"this is literally me."**

- **Avatars are FBI-style profiles**: deepest fears, the exact words they use, what keeps them up at night, what they've already tried, true desires, the content they consume. That's what `/avatars` builds - and wildly different ads work for different avatars.
- **One product, many avatars.** A phone case sells to the outdoorsman, the aesthetic girl, the corporate guy, and the videographer - four entirely different ads, one product. Each avatar changes the tone, the features you highlight, the edit, the music, the feel (the writing side of this lives in [../copywriting/writing_for_your_avatar.md](../copywriting/writing_for_your_avatar.md)).
- **Identification marketing**: sometimes you don't argue at all - you show the product and the avatar in the dream state, and she says "yeah, this is me. I want to be this."

Next to desire, understanding your customer is the second - maybe even the first - most important concept in marketing. How to actually build that understanding: [research_methods.md](research_methods.md).
