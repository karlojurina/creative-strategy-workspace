# How Ads Work - The Game You're Playing

The foundation doc. Before hooks, scripts, or metrics, you need the shape of the whole game: what marketing is, why paid ads, what kind of ads matter, and the seven-step loop every ad goes through.

## Marketing, defined

**Marketing is just getting more people to buy a thing.** That's the whole definition. Everything else is method.

Three ways to do it online: content, email, and paid ads. Paid wins on all three axes that matter - **fast** (make an ad today, run it today, people buy today), **measurable** (every step tracked), and **scalable** (found a winner? Pour more money behind it). How it works end to end: the brand makes an ad -> pays a platform to show it -> some people see it, some click, some buy -> the brand makes back more than it put in. Once you crack it, it pretty much becomes a money printer.

And when people say "scale an e-commerce brand," in 99% of cases they mean scaling the ads.

## Why this skill matters right now

Commerce as a thing isn't going anywhere - people will always buy and sell. What's changing is where it happens and how much: billions of people on social media for hours a day, trillions spent online every year, the whole world entering the consumer economy. **In this story, ads are the shovels.** Brands need them the way gold miners needed shovels and pickaxes - and you're the one making them. It's also the entry point with no capital, no inventory, no suppliers, and no backend risk: most e-commerce brands, especially dropshipping brands, are really marketing businesses - that's ~90% of their results.

## Direct response vs branded

**Branded advertising** (Nike billboards, Super Bowl spots) builds awareness over years and asks for nothing today. **Branded advertising is a rich company game** - DTC brands earn the right to it around eight to nine figures a year.

**Direct response** asks people to do something RIGHT NOW: clear offer, strong call to action, every step measurable - saw it, stopped, clicked, bought. It's the only kind of advertising that really matters for the brands we work with.

The craft twist: the point of direct response ads today is **to not look like ads.** The anatomy that works: relatable open -> problem education -> the techniques/mechanism -> the product - so by the end the viewer thinks "oh shit, I need a humidifier" without ever feeling pitched.

## The seven-step loop

Every ad is made the same way, and the loop never ends:

```
research -> strategy -> copywriting & briefing -> production
    ^                                                |
    |                                                v
iteration <- analyzing (learnings) <- testing (launch)
```

1. **Research** - understand the product, market, competitors, and audience before anything else. Always first.
2. **Strategy** - pick the avatar, the desire, the angle, the awareness play (this is your growth guide card's what/why/how).
3. **Copywriting & briefing** - write the script, assemble the brief.
4. **Production** - editors and designers execute it.
5. **Testing** - launch and let the market vote.
6. **Analyzing** - learnings, on winners AND losers: analyze the losers "so that we can understand literally, why do we suck." From every single ad, win or lose, you learn something.
7. **Iteration** - feed the learning into the next batch. Rinse and repeat.

**Who owns what:** the creative strategist owns research, strategy, copy, briefing, and iteration; editors and designers own production; media buyers own testing; analyzing is done together as a team. And the dependency truth: performance depends mostly on research, strategy, copy, and briefing - but all of that falls into the water if production executes it badly.

## The testing doctrine

Brands need to test A LOT of ads to find winners - not every ad you make will win, and that is the system working, not failing. Turn off the losing ads, keep the winning ads, learn from both. The unit of testing is the **batch** (four hook variations of one ad - see [../copywriting/hooks.md](../copywriting/hooks.md)), the tracker is your growth guide, the verdicts and diagnostics live in the [learnings playbook](../learnings-playbook.md), and what to build after a verdict lives in the [iteration playbook](../iteration-playbook.md).

Aim for **3-4 strong angles - not one perfect one, not twenty mediocre ones.**
