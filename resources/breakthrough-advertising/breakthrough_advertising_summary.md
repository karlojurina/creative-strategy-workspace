# Breakthrough Advertising - The Summary

A chapter-by-chapter summary of Eugene Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising*. This is the reference the skills pull from when they need an awareness call, a sophistication call, or one of the seven techniques. It is NOT a substitute for the book - read the real thing, more than once (see the [README](README.md) for how to add your copy here).

## The core premise

**The power that makes advertising work comes from the market itself, not from the copy.** Copy cannot create desire for a product - it can only channel and direct desires that already exist in the prospect's mind. "Let's get right down to the heart of the matter. The power, the force, the overwhelming urge to own that makes advertising work, comes from the market itself, and not from the copy."

When advertising taps already-existing desire, $1 of ad spend can produce $50-100 in sales (the Amplification Effect). When it tries to create desire from scratch, $1 in produces at best $1 out.

**The copywriter's three core tasks:**
1. **Choose the most powerful desire** that can be applied to your product - the one with the greatest urgency, staying power, and scope. Only one desire can predominate in your headline.
2. **Acknowledge that desire and offer the means to satisfy it in your headline** - the headline is the bridge between prospect and product, touching the prospect at their current stage of awareness.
3. **Show how the product's performance satisfies that desire** - demonstrate the inevitable path from the product's features to the desire's fulfillment.

**Two aspects of every product:**
- The **physical product** - the material object the manufacturer is proud of.
- The **functional product** - the product in action, the benefits it delivers. This is what people actually buy. "People do not buy the steel in a car, the glass in a vase, the tobacco in a cigarette, or the paper in a book."

Physical attributes still matter - to justify the price, document performance quality, assure continued performance, sharpen the mental picture, and provide a fresh basis for believability (the point of difference).

# Part 1 - The Basic Strategy of Persuasion

## Chapter 1: Mass Desire

**Mass desire = the public spread of a private want.** A market is born the moment a private desire is shared by a statistically significant number of people willing to pay to satisfy it.

**Three dimensions of mass desire** (how to weigh which desire to build on):
1. **Urgency** - intensity of the demand for satisfaction (constant arthritic pain > minor headache).
2. **Staying power** - repetition and inability to be satiated (raw hunger > gourmet craving).
3. **Scope** - how many people share it.

**The forces that create mass desire:**
- *Permanent forces:* **Mass instinct** (women wanting to be attractive, men virile, everyone healthy - your job is distinguishing your product with fresh appeals and stronger believability) and **mass technological problems** (ongoing unsolved problems - offer the same relief as competitors, but in a new, more compelling way).
- *Forces of change:* **Trends** (catch rising tides while they're almost imperceptible) and **mass education** (recognize when a shift becomes statistically significant enough to act on).

## Chapter 2: Your Prospect's State of Awareness

**The headline's real job:** not to sell, not to mention the product, not even to state the main appeal. Its only job is to stop your prospect and compel them to read the second sentence. The second sentence's only job is to force the third. The chain runs the whole ad.

**The five states of awareness** - each separated from the next by a psychological wall; a headline written for one stage fails completely at another:

| Stage | The prospect | Headline strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Most aware** | Knows your product, knows what it does, wants it - just hasn't bought yet | Product name + bargain price ("Revere Zomar Lens - Formerly $149.50 - Now Only $119.95") |
| **2. Product-aware** | Knows your product exists, isn't convinced yet | Product name prominent; headline points out superiority - reinforce desire, sharpen image, extend usage, new proof, new mechanism, remove limitations, or reposition out of the competition ("At 60 miles an hour, the loudest noise in this Rolls Royce is the electric clock") |
| **3. Solution-aware** | Wants what the product does, doesn't know YOUR product does it | Name the desire and/or its solution ("How to win friends and influence people") - then prove it can be done and show your product contains the mechanism |
| **4. Problem-aware** | Has the need, doesn't know a product fulfills it | Name the problem and/or its solution ("Corns?", "Shrinks hemorrhoids without surgery", "Do YOU make these mistakes in English?") - dramatize the need, then present the product as the inevitable solution |
| **5. Completely unaware** | Doesn't recognize the desire, won't admit it, or it can't fit in a headline | Indirect: an identification headline that echoes an emotion or attitude, defines the prospect to themselves, and sells nothing but reading on ("Why Men Crack...", "They Laughed When I Sat Down at the Piano", "Often a Bridesmaid but Never a Bride"). No price, no product name, no direct claims |

## Chapter 3: The Sophistication of Your Market

**Market sophistication = how many similar products has this market already been told about?** It decides what kind of claim can still work.

| Stage | Situation | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | You're first with this product | Be simple and direct: name the need or the claim, nothing more ("NOW! LOSE UGLY FAT!") - nothing more is needed |
| **2** | Second in, direct claim still works | Copy the claim and ENLARGE it - drive it to the limit, outbid the competition ("LOSE UP TO 47 POUNDS IN 4 WEEKS - OR RECEIVE $40 BACK!"). Eventually believability runs out: words lose meaning, prospects discount claims, sales turn down |
| **3** | Market has heard all claims in all their extremes | **A new mechanism** - a new way to make the old claims believable again ("FLOATS FAT RIGHT OUT OF YOUR BODY!"). The headline now features the mechanism (HOW it works), with the claim beneath |
| **4** | A competitor just launched a new mechanism | Elaborate and enlarge on the mechanism - easier, quicker, surer, fewer limitations, extra benefits ("FIRST NO-DIET REDUCING WONDER DRUG!") - the same escalation curve as stage 2 |
| **5** | Market exhausted, no claim believed | Identification advertising - shift focus from the product to the prospect (the Marlboro man). Same strategy as awareness stage 5 |

**Case study - cigarettes through all five stages:** simple pleasure claims ("I'D WALK A MILE FOR A CAMEL") -> enlarged claims ("LIGHT UP A LUCKY AND YOU WON'T MISS THE SWEETS THAT MAKE YOU FAT") -> mechanisms ("LUCKIES - THEY'RE TOASTED!", "PROTECT YOUR T-ZONE") -> elaborated mechanisms ("ALL THE HARSHNESS BAKED OUT", "NINE OUT OF TEN DOCTORS PREFER LUCKIES") -> pure identification (the Marlboro virile-men campaign, no claims at all).

## Chapter 4: 38 Ways to Strengthen Your Headline

**Verbalization** - increasing a headline's impact through how it's stated: strengthen the claim, make it fresh again, pull the prospect into the body. The 38 techniques, one example each:

1. **Measure the size of the claim** - "WHO EVER HEARD OF 17,000 BLOOMS FROM A SINGLE PLANT?"
2. **Measure the speed** - "IN TWO SECONDS, BAYER ASPIRIN BEGINS TO DISSOLVE IN YOUR GLASS!"
3. **Compare the claim** - "SIX TIMES WHITER WASHES!"
4. **Metaphorize it** - "MELTS AWAY UGLY FAT!"
5. **Sensitize it** (make them feel/smell/touch/see/hear it) - "THE SKIN YOU LOVE TO TOUCH!"
6. **Demonstrate it with a prime example** - "AT 60 MILES AN HOUR, THE LOUDEST NOISE IN THIS ROLLS ROYCE IS THE ELECTRIC CLOCK!"
7. **Dramatize the claim or its result** - "THEY LAUGHED WHEN I SAT DOWN AT THE PIANO - BUT WHEN I STARTED TO PLAY..."
8. **State it as a paradox** - "HOW A BALD-HEADED BARBER HELPED SAVE MY HAIR!"
9. **Remove limitations** - "SHRINKS HEMORRHOIDS WITHOUT SURGERY!"
10. **Associate with desired values/people** - "9 OUT OF 10 DECORATORS USE WUNDAWEAVE CARPETS"
11. **Show how much work it does, in detail** - "RELIEVES CONGESTION IN ALL 7 NASAL PASSAGES INSTANTLY!"
12. **State it as a question** - "WHO ELSE WANTS A WHITER WASH - WITH NO HARD WORK?"
13. **Offer information on how to accomplish it** - "HOW TO WIN FRIENDS AND INFLUENCE PEOPLE"
14. **Tie in authority** - "HERE'S WHAT DOCTORS DO WHEN THEY FEEL ROTTEN!"
15. **Before-and-after** - the Coldene 5-days-of-suffering vs 5-days-recovered pair
16. **Stress newness** - "ANNOUNCING! GUIDED MISSILE SPARK PLUGS!"
17. **Stress exclusivity** - "ONLY GLEEM HAS GL-70..."
18. **Turn it into a challenge** - "WHICH TWIN HAS THE TONI?"
19. **State it as a case-history quotation** - "LOOK, MOM - NO CAVITIES!"
20. **Condense it - swap your product for what it replaces** - "NOW! A RING AND PISTON JOB IN A TUBE!"
21. **Symbolize it via a parallel reality** - "STARTING NEXT TUESDAY, THE ATLANTIC OCEAN BECOMES ONLY ONE-FIFTH AS LONG!"
22. **Connect mechanism to claim** - "FLOATS FAT RIGHT OUT OF YOUR BODY!"
23. **Startle by contradicting how the mechanism should work** - "'HIT HELL OUT OF THE BALL WITH YOUR RIGHT HAND,' SAYS TOMMY ARMOUR!"
24. **Connect the need and the claim** - "THERE IS ONLY ONE SOLUTION TO AN ADVERTISING PROBLEM: FIND THE MAN!"
25. **Offer information in the ad itself** - "WHY MEN CRACK..."
26. **Turn claim or need into a case history** - "AGAIN SHE ORDERS - A CHICKEN SALAD, PLEASE!"
27. **Give the problem a name** - "WHEN YOU'RE WEARY WITH DAY-TIME FATIGUE, TAKE ALKA-SELTZER"
28. **Warn about pitfalls of not using it** - "DON'T INVEST ONE CENT... UNTIL YOU CHECK THIS GUIDE!"
29. **Emphasize by phraseology** (break in two, repeat) - "NOBODY BUT NOBODY UNDERSELLS GIMBEL'S!"
30. **Show how easy via a universally-overcome limitation** - "IF YOU CAN COUNT TO ELEVEN..."
31. **State the difference** - "THE DIFFERENCE IN PREMIUM GASOLINES IS RIGHT IN THE ADDITIVES!"
32. **Surprise that former limitations are overcome** - "SEE WHAT HAPPENS WHEN YOU CRUSH A HARTMAN DC-8? NOTHING!"
33. **Address the people who CAN'T buy** - "IF YOU'VE ALREADY TAKEN YOUR VACATION, DON'T READ THIS..."
34. **Address your prospect directly** - "TO THE MAN WHO WILL SETTLE FOR NOTHING LESS THAN THE PRESIDENCY OF HIS FIRM"
35. **Dramatize how hard it was to produce** - "WHEN JENS FINISHED DESIGNING THIS CANDLEHOLDER WE HAD TO INVENT A WHOLE NEW KIND OF CANDLE"
36. **Accuse the claim of being too good** - "IS IT IMMORAL TO MAKE MONEY THIS EASILY?"
37. **Challenge the prospect's limiting beliefs** - "YOU ARE TWICE AS SMART AS YOU THINK"
38. **Turn it into question and answer** - the United Delco "who should you see" ad

## Chapter 5: The Art of Creative Planning

**Three levels of creativity:**
1. **Word-substitute** (shallowest) - swap your product into someone else's successful headline. "Echo ads" - they ignore the unique product-market-timing relationship.
2. **Formula approach** - apply memorized rules. Works for verbalization, fails at core ideas: "the idea for an ad demands its own shape."
3. **Analytical approach** (the breakthrough technique) - guidepost QUESTIONS, not fixed answers. "Analysis is the art of asking the right questions and letting the problem dictate the right answers."

Other planning tools:
- **Research findings are directions, not headlines.** First they tell you where NOT to go; turning facts into an idea still takes the full creative work.
- **Product personality:** every product has a summary trait in the consumer's mind (Cadillac = quality). Feature it; make other traits strengthen it, never compete with it.
- **Prevention doesn't sell to the self.** People won't visualize future disasters happening to THEM - focus the prevention on their loved ones (cavities on the kids, life insurance on the family).
- **Splinter markets:** smaller companies can own a segment (sociological, not just geographic) - the reducing aid that abandoned "appearance" to own "health." Success in the niche later becomes the point of difference for expansion.

**The creative process:** market analysis -> product study -> the theme (the desire your market demands + its satisfaction) -> market maturity to find the entry point -> the headline (5-10 words carrying ~90% of the ad's value). **Cardinal rule:** the solution lies inside the product-market-timing relationship, nowhere else. No outside formula will hand it to you.

# Part 2 - The Seven Techniques

## Chapter 6: Inside Your Prospect's Mind

Three dimensions you work with:
1. **Desires** - physical, material, sensual wants. Your task: sharpen desire with vivid mental pictures of satisfaction.
2. **Identifications** - the roles the prospect wants to play and traits they want to project. Your task: make them feel the prestige of using the product; show them who uses it.
3. **Beliefs** - opinions, attitudes, prejudices, their filter for all information. Your task: build FROM their existing beliefs, with their logic.

**Ad length** = however much copy it takes to (1) build desire to full strength, (2) create comfortable identification, (3) overcome objections and build belief. No more, no less.

## Chapter 7: Intensification

Expanding and magnifying desire by presenting concrete images of satisfaction. Desire sits in the prospect's mind vague and directionless - you fill it out with pictures. "The sharper you can draw your pictures - and the greater the number of them - the more your prospect will demand your product, and the less important will seem your price."

**Thirteen ways to strengthen desire:**
1. **Direct presentation of claims** - vivid, thorough description of results
2. **Put the claims in action** - not just what it does, exactly HOW it does it
3. **Bring in the reader** - put them in the story ("pull up to another car at the stop light... then slam your foot down on the gas pedal!")
4. **Show testing** - let them visualize proving it themselves
5. **Stretch benefits in time** - weeks and months of continuous benefit
6. **Bring in an audience** - testimonials, case histories, observers
7. **Show expert approval** - with surprise, competition, discovery
8. **Compare, contrast, prove superiority** - old product's flaws beside the new one's strengths
9. **Picture the black side** - paint the problem full black before applying the salve
10. **Show how easy it is** - simplicity of use against magnitude of benefit
11. **Metaphor, analogy, imagination** - make abstract benefits concrete
12. **Summarize before you're done** - the catalog technique (horizontal: more applications; vertical: deeper desire)
13. **Put the guarantee to work** - make it the climax, a final summary of performance, not just risk reversal

## Chapter 8: Identification

Building a saleable personality into the product that matches how prospects want to define themselves. "People don't change: only the direction of their desires do."

**Two types of roles prospects want:**
- **Character roles** (adjectives): "chic," "brilliant," "well-read." Products help achieve the role, speed it up, or symbolize it for acknowledgment. Hard to measure = easy to accept without questioning.
- **Achievement roles** (titles): "Executive," "Home Owner," "Good Mother." Invisible achievements that need physical symbols - products become status definers.

**How to use it:** start from the primary image the product already has (cigarettes = virile), then either intensify it (Marlboro: virile men, virile situations, tattoos) or use it as a bridge to a more favorable image (Chesterfield's "Blow Some My Way": kept the smoking man, subdued him, led to romance). **Limits:** prospects reject unrealistic identifications - you build bridges from what they already accept, never force past it.

## Chapter 9: Gradualization

Building a bridge of belief from facts the prospect already accepts to the facts you want them to believe. **"If you violate your prospect's established beliefs in the slightest degree - either in content or direction - then nothing you promise him, no matter how appealing, can save your ad."**

Every claim has two sources of strength: its content, and the PREPARATION made for it in the preceding copy. Effective ads create a "stream of acceptances" leading to conviction. The five awareness stages are really stages of *readiness to accept* - which is why the most powerful claim doesn't always make the most powerful headline.

**Belief-building devices:**
1. **Inclusion questions** - questions that permit immediate identification ("Do you find it increasingly difficult to cope...")
2. **Detailed identification** - cataloging their symptoms so they know you understand them
3. **Contradiction of present false beliefs** - only with strong authority and an immediate alternative
4. **The language of logic** - "the reason," "therefore," "simply because," "the fact that"
5. **Syllogistic thinking** - chains where each statement leads inevitably to the next
6. Contingency structures ("if... then..."), repetition of proof by echoing, promise-belief-promise variation, paragraph parallelism

Classic example: the TV repair manual. The direct claim ("Save up to $100 a Year on TV Repairs") failed; "WHY HAVEN'T TV OWNERS BEEN TOLD THESE FACTS?" tapped universal resentment, built a habit of agreement with inclusion questions, described universal symptoms, held the mechanism back until the reader demanded it - and only then introduced the idea that owners could do repairs themselves.

## Chapter 10: Redefinition

Removing objections by redefining the product so drawbacks become assets or irrelevant.

| Drawback | Solution | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Seems too complicated | **Simplification** | TV manual: "repairs" redefined as "minor adjustments on outside controls"; "your TV set is a great deal like your body" - symptoms, not breakdowns |
| Not important enough | **Escalation** | English course: from grammar-correctness to "hold others spellbound with the power of your speech"; or show what hinges on it ("a single 99-cent part robs you of the real power of your $4,000 car") |
| Costs too much | **Price reduction by comparison** | $1.49 spark plugs vs $5 hand-made ones; "pay for themselves 12 times in one year"; stack bargain-reinforcing phrases |

Multiple techniques stack: Schwartz counts 10 separate bargain-reinforcing phrases in two paragraphs of the spark plug ad.

## Chapter 11: Mechanization

Verbally proving the product does what you claim by demonstrating HOW it works. The prospect runs a silent dialogue with your copy: "Tell me more." "Oh yeah? Who says so?" "How does it work?" - answer each demand at exactly the right moment.

**Three stages of mechanism:**
1. **Name it** (well-known mechanisms) - catalog copy: "With Electronic Light Setter... Only $135."
2. **Describe it** (less familiar) - the Promise -> Reason Why formula (Rinso: promise a whiter wash, then "the secret is simply Rinso - a mild granulated soap that gives rich, lasting suds..."). Sell the mechanism as hard as the claim.
3. **Feature it** (sophisticated markets, stage 3) - the mechanism becomes the headline: "FLOATS FAT RIGHT OUT OF YOUR BODY."

Even price cuts need a mechanism - the Robert Collier overstocked-mill letter made the discount believable by explaining WHY it existed.

## Chapter 12: Concentration

Proving that ALTERNATE ways of satisfying the desire are ineffective compared to your product. Attacking a competitor evokes dislike UNLESS the attack serves the reader: "If you can show your customer that this attack is for his own good... because your product will eliminate this weakness, then you have a sales story he will accept."

**Two structures:**
- **Direct contrast** (bad-good, bad-good): "A spark plug jumps a spark..." vs "A fire injector fires on..." - parallel sentence structure sharpening every contrast.
- **Time sequence** (before-after): the reducing ad walking through every failed passive plan, the snapped willpower, the fat flowing back - then the new sequence with the product.

Concentration can be a full copy block or three words: "SHRINKS HEMORRHOIDS WITHOUT SURGERY" carries an implied attack on every alternative.

## Chapter 13: Camouflage

Borrowing believability from sources the prospect already trusts, by adopting their formats, phraseology, and tone. People trust their publications - blend in and borrow the trust.

1. **Format adaptation** - a Wall Street Journal ad set in Journal type, Journal layout, line drawing instead of photo: "about twice the believability, twice the pulling power" of the same copy done the slick way.
2. **Phraseology adaptation** - news style ("SKIN SPECIALIST DEMONSTRATES..."), important-correspondence style for direct mail, documentary tone for broadcast.
3. **Mood adaptation** - break the hard-sell stereotype: **understatement** (the Volkswagen ads - short sentences that fall rather than rise, self-deprecating humor) and **deadly sincerity** (pointing out the offer's own limits: "This is a private advertisement. It is not meant for ninety-nine men out of every hundred.")

## Chapter 14: The Final Touches

- **Verification** - proof (stats, tests, testimonials, authorities) placed at the moment the reader unconsciously demands it; as short, dramatic, and specific as possible. Proof is also selling copy.
- **Reinforcement** - in emotional writing 1+1 can equal 10: two images properly joined ("We're only second" + "We try harder") outpull either alone. Frankly admitting a flaw makes the following benefit far more credible.
- **Interweaving** - pack promise, image, and logic into every sentence (the fire-injector sentence stacking six disadvantages of the old, then five promises of the new).
- **Sensitivity** - sense when the reader is satiated with one kind of copy and wants a shift; different ads need different architectures (Promise -> Definition -> Documentation -> Promise; Authority -> Promise -> Concentration -> Promise; ...). The key skill: feeling when copy has "gone dead" and changing direction.
- **Momentum** - momentum phrases ("Here's how.", "And yet it's only the beginning.", "For example -") and incomplete statements/teasers that raise a question and imply the answer is coming.
- **Mood** - words and rhythms set the emotion: color words for visual products, staccato and power words for momentum, adjective-free straight talk for sincerity. Read the copy aloud.

# Applying Schwartz's methods in modern contexts

Schwartz wrote in the 1960s; the principles hold across every medium today:

- **Email subject lines** - the 5 awareness states apply directly: most-aware subscribers need only product name and price; unaware prospects need identification headlines.
- **Landing pages** - follow the gradualization sequence, building belief systematically from the headline through every content block.
- **Social media** - use identification techniques to create shareable content that helps prospects express their desired roles.
- **Search advertising** - match copy to search-term awareness: informational searches = problem-aware, brand searches = product-aware.
- **Video content** - structure scripts with intensification, reinforcing the same desire from multiple perspectives.

**Campaign integration:** maintain the central campaign idea across platforms, use different verbalization per channel, keep the product personality consistent across touchpoints, build belief through graduated exposure, and apply the right format camouflage to each medium.

**Adaptations for today's market psychology:** consumers are more sophisticated and skeptical overall; markets move through the sophistication stages faster; many markets sit in perpetual fifth-stage sophistication; digital tools allow precise splinter-market targeting; and testing makes the refinement of appeals scientific.

# Testing Schwartz's principles

Schwartz was a strong advocate of testing to determine what works:

- **A/B test headlines** written for different awareness stages.
- **Test copy length** - how much intensification does YOUR market need?
- **Measure mechanism impact** - the same ad with and without the detailed mechanism.
- **Evaluate identification elements** - different personality traits emphasized.
- **Test gradualization sequences** - different arrangements of claims and proofs.

# The ethics of these techniques

These techniques work because they tap real human desires and beliefs - which is exactly why they carry responsibility:

- Intensification becomes manipulation when the promised satisfaction can't be delivered.
- Identification becomes harmful when it exploits insecurities instead of fulfilling aspirations.
- Gradualization must lead to honest conclusions, not mislead through logical fallacies.
- Redefinition must genuinely reframe, never misrepresent.
- Mechanization must explain real processes, not pseudoscience.

Schwartz's own frame: channel existing desires toward legitimate satisfaction, respect the prospect's intelligence, build on established beliefs rather than shattering them, present honest proof, and create advertising that stands the test of time through satisfied customers. The highest form of copy matches genuine solutions to genuine needs.

# The evolution of Breakthrough Advertising

- **The direct response revolution** - Schwartz's techniques formed the backbone of modern direct response; many "gurus" repackaged these concepts without attribution; digital marketing's measurability brought renewed focus to them.
- **Integration with brand building** - product personality (Identification) became central to brand strategy; the best advertising builds both immediate response and long-term brand value.
- **From mass marketing to personalization** - technology now allows individualized awareness and sophistication assessment; dynamic content can deliver calibrated gradualization sequences at scale.

# Mastering the techniques

1. **Study** - analyze successful advertising the way Schwartz did, looking beneath the surface for structural patterns.
2. **Deconstruction** - break ads down by technique to understand how they build persuasion.
3. **Practice** - apply specific techniques consciously and deliberately until they become intuitive.
4. **Testing** - learn from market response which approaches work for your products and audiences.
5. **Synthesis** - develop your own integrated approach combining these principles.

# The revolutionary nature of Schwartz's approach

- **From the product to the market** - traditional advertising centered on product features; Schwartz centered on existing market desires, which is what makes the Amplification Effect possible.
- **From clever ads to effective ads** - he measured value solely by sales results; creativity anchored to measurable outcomes.
- **From intuitive creativity to analytical creativity** - systematic frameworks applicable to any product-market situation, giving replicable success instead of hit-or-miss inspiration.
- **From broadcasting messages to building persuasion architectures** - comprehensive structures that guide prospects through precisely calibrated emotional and logical steps.

**Why the principles endure:** people remain driven by the same core desires (physical, social, emotional - the expressions evolve, the motivations persist); prospects still move through predictable awareness stages, markets through sophistication stages, and belief still precedes buying; and desire, identification, and belief remain the three dimensions that determine buying behavior across generations.

# The complete process, in order

1. **Market analysis** - identify the mass desire; determine awareness stage (1-5); assess sophistication stage (1-5).
2. **Product analysis** - physical vs functional; the performance that best satisfies the dominant desire; the point of difference; the existing product personality.
3. **Headline** - chosen by awareness + sophistication, strengthened by verbalization, acceptable at first glance as true.
4. **Body structure** - a strategic progression of promises, claims, and proof that anticipates every question before it's asked.
5. **The seven techniques** - intensify desire, build identification, gradualize belief, redefine objections, mechanize claims, concentrate on alternatives' weaknesses, camouflage in trusted formats.
6. **Final touches** - verify, reinforce, interweave, keep momentum, set the mood.

# The ultimate lesson

> "Every new market - every new product - every new advertisement is a fresh new problem that never existed before on the face of this earth. Past advertising successes can only serve us as guideposts - as indicators of general directions. They can never solve today's specific problems for us."

And on what the job actually is: "We are all primarily conceptual midwives, helping give birth to new markets for our products. All the other functions we or our businesses perform - the manufacturing, distribution, service, financing, and all the rest - are simply adjuncts to this vital central process." Advertising's essential function is not merely to sell products but to create and develop markets - the deepest truth Schwartz uncovered.

The frameworks never write the ad for you - they are the questions you ask while YOU see the situation with fresh eyes. "The true breakthrough in advertising comes not from following formulas but from seeing each situation with fresh eyes, armed with timeless principles of human psychology and persuasion."
