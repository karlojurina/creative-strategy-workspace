# Eugene Schwartz Video Notes

Notes taken 18-03-2025 from a ~1.5 hour video of Eugene Schwartz talking about his workflow, marketing, and ads. Watching it after reading Breakthrough Advertising twice produced the breakthrough that reshaped how we write copy (the story is in [the FlowPouch case study](../winning-ads/flowpouch_story.md)). Quotes are his words; the rest is the note-taker's shorthand.

## General mentality

- Success doesn't come from being creative or talented but simply putting in the work.
- "I want to be more accurate and more knowledgeable than anyone I come up against."
- **"THE PERSON WHO IS THE MOST PREPARED AND MOST KNOWLEDGEABLE MAKES THE MOST MONEY."**
- 2 out of 3 times, the sloppiness of a "great copywriter" will be beaten by the carefulness of a "mid copywriter" - attention to detail and work ethic beat ego and talent.
- "You have much more talented copywriters working for your firm, but there is nobody in your firm that works harder than I do."

## His research and writing process

- When studying a manuscript he skips the table of contents and goes through the book first page to last. He's not interested in what's in the book - he's interested in the book itself. He wants to be MOVED by it. As he reads, he writes ad ideas on a piece of paper - nothing complicated, just stored somewhere.
- He reads only 1 hour a day (more is too much). He goes through the manuscript **looking for claims** - highlighting the claims on every page, then extracting all of them into one place.
- Then he starts writing the ad - no headlines, no subheadlines yet - he picks the strongest claims.
- He works 3 hours a day, and it's enough, because he's better prepared than anyone else.

## Beating the blank page

- Old zen master trick: walk into the same place where you always work (he wrote at the same desk for 28 years).
- His timer: 33.33 minutes. One to two minutes of boredom, then he starts going through the copy; the timer rings, 5 minute break.
- The whole secret is knowing when you're WORKING and when you're CREATING.

## Copy is assembled, not written

- "Copy is not written. Copy is assembled. You're building from a series of building blocks, and you're putting them in different structures. You're building a little city of desire for your person to come live in. You're assembling claims, images, desires, that people will pay money to share with you."
- **See through the words** - words in advertising are like the windows in a store: the reader must see right through them to the product. Copy should never call attention to itself - it should create a visceral image of what the words convey.
- Example: *"Sneaky Little Arthritis Tricks"* - a person carrying the pain and humiliation of arthritis, who has spent hundreds of hours with doctors, knows EXACTLY what that headline is talking about.

## Creativity = connectivity

- "Creativity" has no clear visual image - replace it with **connectivity**, an operational word. You're connecting two things that logically wouldn't go together until that moment. You're looking for a brand new connection.
- The mind holds ~7 words in a thought at a time; around them sits a huge cloud of unfocused words. Focused, you can only reach the logical words. To be creative you need to be UNFOCUSED - that's the reason for 33 minutes of work, 5 of play. The brain connects when it's unfocused.
- Mozart never rewrote his music - he had a simple system for escaping the focused mind.
- **Never work on just one thing at a time.** Simultaneous projects feed one another: your unconscious works on one while your conscious works on the other.

## Headlines (from the 44-minute mark)

- Headlines are expected to have tremendous power - wrong. **The headline's job is simply to be tantalizing enough to get the reader to read the next line.** It should not sell, define the brand, or present overwhelming power.
- It gets the reader into the habit of reading line after line - and then desiring to read more. Then the copy AS A WHOLE inspires the action (open the envelope, click the link).
- Advertising copy is not paragraphs - it is **a series of headlines**.

## The language of the avatar

- Your copy needs to be **YOUful** - not youthful, full of *yous*.
- You must know the person you're about to ask for something (the purchase).
- Listen on several layers at once: the product, the avatar, society in general.
- Warning: get away from sophistication. When you become successful you risk falling out of touch. Keep up with your audience - their realities, feelings, fears.
- Copy should speak to desire - many people have shameful desires. Heaven-and-hell analogy: be melodramatic and polarizing, while also honest and fair.

## Claim vs mechanism

- As a company grows it runs into **claim exhaustion** - everything starts looking the same. At that point the copy must make up for the claim.
- Include an **implied mechanism** the reader will want to see: *"Think you can't cash an unsigned check? Wrong - see page 75."*
- Can't-be-dones / "the impossible" work best for medium-sized claims - people want to prove it can't be achieved: *"Unspoken rules that can make or break your career."*

## And finally

- "You have to monitor your tests very closely... and your results."
