# Learnings Playbook

How we close the loop on an ad. Every ad that finishes testing gets one of the four verdicts, a learning written into its concept card, and the durable lesson distilled up into the brand's `learnings/learnings.md`.

## The four verdicts (week 1)

We call these the IDGAF definitions: the verdict does not care how much you loved the ad. Two questions decide everything after the first week of testing - how much of the campaign's spend did the ad capture, and what happened to ROAS and budget because of it?

### Spend winner
- Captures **10-30% or more** of total campaign spend *(the higher the campaign's spend, the lower the % needed - and the other way around)*
- Campaign budget has **NOT** increased week over week as a result of launching the ad

### KPI winner
- Does **not** capture 10-30% of spend
- But its ROAS is the **same as or higher than** the initial campaign ROAS

### Loser
- Captures **less than 10-30%** of total campaign spend
- ROAS is **below** the initial campaign ROAS

### Breakthrough
- Captures **10-30% or more** of total campaign spend
- Campaign budget **HAS** increased week over week as a result of launching the ad *(a 10% up to 100%+ budget increase)*

**Remember:** an ad can be labelled a spend winner or a KPI winner in week 1 and become a breakthrough in week 2 or 3 - as long as it keeps pulling its share of spend and the daily budget keeps climbing. When that happens, upgrade the card's verdict and stamp the date in its Timeline.

## The five scenarios after your ad goes live

Every ad you put up lands in one of five scenarios. Before reading them, three pieces of context that apply to all five:

1. **KPI** - every brand has a target, usually ROAS. Above it = "hitting KPI", the brand makes money and keeps the ad running. Below it = "missing KPI", the brand loses money on every dollar the ad spends.
2. **The brand doesn't decide what gets spent on your ad. Meta does.** Most brands run CBO campaigns: one daily budget, every active ad competing for the same pool, Meta's algorithm deciding daily who gets fed. No human in the loop, no favoritism. If the ad is good, Meta spends on it. The system is honest like that.
3. **Spend is unpredictable.** An ad can sit at $5 for five days and explode on day six, or spend $3K on day one and die by day three. Watch the trend, not the moment.

### Scenario 1 - Your ad isn't getting spend

Meta tested it on a small group, nobody engaged, and it stopped pushing. There is only one question: **why does nobody care about this ad?** Watch the first 4 seconds the way a stranger would - forget you made it. If you wouldn't stop, the algorithm wouldn't either. The fix is almost always the opening: make the first second visually scream who it's for (a specific problem, a specific person, a specific moment they recognize from their own life), and make the first line sharpen the signal. Two goals only: **impossible to scroll past, impossible to misread who it's for.** Re-shoot the opening, launch it as a new variation. And sometimes an ad just never takes off - take the learning and ship the next one instead of agonizing.

### Scenario 2 - Hitting KPI but not getting enough spend (the kpi winner)

The few people who see it buy, but Meta won't scale it. Three usual causes: the **hook** isn't stopping enough people (converts the ones who stop, but too few stop); the **audience signal is too narrow** (the ad's visuals and copy point Meta at a slice too small to scale); or the **bridge from hook to body is broken** (seconds 4-10 lose the people the hook stopped). Fix the one the numbers point at: rewrite the hook, broaden the angle, or make the hook-to-body transition seamless. This is a great place to be - conversion is proven, it just needs more eyeballs.

### Scenario 3 - Spending but missing KPI

Meta found the audience, the hook worked, but nobody pulls out their wallet. **This is not a reach problem. It's a belief problem.** Usual causes: the claims don't land as believable; the ad-to-landing-page gap (the ad pitches one angle, the page pitches another, the viewer feels the disconnect and leaves); or you opened loops you didn't close (they finished the ad unsatisfied instead of resolved). Fixes: add proof (real results, real testimonials, evidence over claim); click your own ad and read the landing page like a viewer - does it hand them the same emotional moment the ad set up?; close every loop the ad opens. And check for urgency - most ads that miss KPI also miss it. Surface the real cost of not buying now; don't fabricate one.

### Scenario 4 - Spending well, then dies

It printed for days, then fell off a cliff. What usually happened: it was a bottom-of-funnel ad that converted the warm pool, then Meta expanded to cold traffic - and cold audiences don't buy logical pitches, they buy emotional ones. The fix is to **move the ad up the awareness ladder**: open with a problem moment instead of a product shot, visualize the desire instead of listing stats, sell the feeling instead of the feature. Make a cold viewer feel "wait, that's me" in the first 3 seconds. Also worth checking before you blame yourself: seasonal demand shifts, seasonal visuals that stopped matching the viewer's life, a competitor's stronger creative, or plain audience saturation. The rewrite is still the right first move every time - but if it doesn't revive the angle, take the learning and move on.

### Scenario 5 - Spending, hitting KPI, and scaling (the breakthrough)

The ad starts eating the campaign's daily pool - 60, 70, 80% of spend - and when it keeps holding, the brand scales the entire campaign budget because your ad is carrying it. Celebrate it. Then do not walk away: **a winning ad is the start, not the end.** Study the winner first - watch it like you've never seen it, spend 30 minutes forming 2-3 hypotheses about WHY it's winning (the hook? the angle? the format? the voice?). Then iterate hard: open `resources/iteration-playbook.md`.

## Common practices

- Watch the **first 3 seconds only** for ads that are not spend winners / breakthroughs
- Watch the **entire ad** if the ad is a spend winner / breakthrough
- Go through the **comments** on every ad
- Look at the **soft metrics** on every ad
- Make a doc with the questions we ask about why this is doing well / not doing well - the list below

## The questions (why is this doing well / not doing well)

- What is the **mechanism** here?
- What is the **authority** here - how are we building credibility?
- Are we **VISUALIZING** the desire being fulfilled?
- How are we creating **curiosity** - and when we create curiosity, are we satisfying it?
- Are we opening **new loops** throughout the video (not only in the hook)?
- Are we using **visual language**?
- Are we using **simple to understand** language?
- Are we stating **opinions or facts**?
- Does the ad make you **FEEL** something, or is it purely logical? (we should make people feel something)
- Are we stating something that **no one else can say**?
- Is it clear **how the product works / fulfils the desire**?
- What is the ad **communicating** (reading between the lines)?
- Do we have a mechanism that creates **new hope** for the avatar?
- Does this ad **shorten time, remove effort & sacrifice** to get to the desired outcome?
- What **valence / intensity zone** is this ad in - are we lecturing them or making them feel understood?
- Does this story have **drama** - does it have **high stakes**?

## Application: questions to ask yourself before submitting your brief for video ads

- Does the first 1-2 seconds of this ad hook the RIGHT user in & KEEP them hooked in?
- Is this an ad that your potential customers would want to see?
- Does the copywriting create a clear image / visual in the user's mind?
- Does this video feel native to the platform?
- Does this video contain a label for who the type of person is?
- Would this video make someone smile or laugh?
- Is the video relatable?
- Does this video actually look like an ad?
- Gut check - does this video feel authentic?
- What learnings of previous winning ads have we applied to this ad?
- Does the ad educate the user in a creative way on how to achieve their core desire?
- Have you applied escalation to exaggerate your claim in a clear and relatable way?
- Does this ad either:
  - Tell a story the avatar would find interesting and be emotionally invested in?
  - Entertain the avatar and make them smile, laugh, or share it with a friend?
  - Educate the avatar and introduce them to a new mechanism that is believable?
- Does this ad separate your brand from the competition?
