# Hooks - The First 3 to 5 Seconds

The hook is the first 3-5 seconds of the ad. The hold is everything after. The hook hooks the attention; the hold holds it - and **the hook has the highest impact on the entire ad.** Nothing else you write gets seen if the hook fails.

## The two jobs of the hook

Every hook has exactly two goals, and it must hit both:

1. **Impossible to scroll past.**
2. **Impossible to misread who it's for.**

A hook that stops everyone but signals no one buys you a great hook rate and a dead ad. A hook that signals perfectly but stops no one never gets the chance. The viewer decides in milliseconds, on a lazy, glucose-saving brain, in a feed they opened to be entertained - so the test for every hook is the core question: **how hard does the brain have to work to get this?** Clarity always beats clever.

## What a hook actually does: open a loop

A working hook plants an open question in the viewer's head that the ad promises to close:

- **New information / new mechanism** - "After birth, women have ten times the normal levels of the fat-storing hormone known as prolactin." They've never heard this. They stay.
- **Contradiction of what they know** - "Everything you know about functional mushrooms is based on a biological lie." Now there's a gap between what they believe and what you just said.
- **Agree with them against the whole market** - "Here's why gaining weight postpartum is normal." Every other ad promises fast weight loss; this one says nothing is wrong with you. Pattern interrupt through honesty.
- **Emotional story gap** - "The real reason this aggressive shelter dog couldn't find a home will break your heart." Curiosity BEFORE names: this beat two variations that introduced the dog by name up front. Withhold, then reveal.
- **Call out the villain** - "The entire functional mushroom industry has a dirty secret they don't want you to know."

All five are the same move underneath: a question the viewer now needs answered. (The full pattern library - 38 ways to strengthen a headline - is in the [Breakthrough Advertising summary](../breakthrough-advertising/breakthrough_advertising_summary.md); size, speed, paradox, challenge, before-and-after, and the rest all work as video hooks too.)

## Hooks come in batches

We make ads as batches: **one batch = four variations of the same ad, where the first 3-5 seconds differ and the rest is the same.** Why: testing different hooks gives the entire batch more chances to survive, and when one hook wins you get a free learning - write down WHY it won (see the growth guide card's Learning field).

Practical moves for the variations:

- **Same script, new hook** - the cheapest iteration that exists.
- **Comment-as-hook** - find a real objection or question in your ad comments and use it verbatim as the opener.
- **In medias res** - start in the middle of the moment, no setup.
- **Reframe for a sub-avatar** - same body, opener aimed at a different slice of the audience.

## The visual half

The hook is not just the words. The first frame must look like something they would watch anyway - an organic piece of content, not an ad. A polished-ad look raises the guard ("they're trying to sell me something"); a post look drops it. Make the first second visually scream who it's for: a specific problem, a specific person, a specific moment they recognize from their own life.

## Hook rate: the benchmark and the trap

Hook rate = viewers who watched past 3 seconds / viewers served. **40-50% is good; 60-70% is crazy.** But the caveat matters more than the benchmark: **a higher hook rate does not mean the ad will do better.** Your hook can be extremely good at capturing the WRONG audience - and ads with 20-30% hook rates can still rip because they stop exactly the right people. Judge hooks by what the ad does downstream (spend, KPI), not by the hook rate alone - the [learnings playbook](../learnings-playbook.md) has the full diagnostic.

## Where to get hook ideas

- Your research: the top 20-25 viral hooks in the niche get documented during `/market-research` - steal patterns, not sentences.
- Your ad comments and reviews - real language beats invented language every time.
- The [hook-ideas prompt](../prompts/hook-ideas.md) for generating variations against a finished body.
- Competitor ads that have been running the longest (running time = the market voting).
