# Text on Screen - Writing for the Inner Voice

You know that voice in your head that never shuts up - the one reading these words right now? Text-on-screen ads weaponize it. No voiceover, no creator: just words appearing over video footage. It has printed for multiple brands (a recent winner runs almost two minutes with over 20% hold rate), across supplements, fitness, and pet products - and it works for one psychological reason, which means it has to be WRITTEN differently.

## Why it works

When people watch a UGC video, they hear someone else selling to them. It's obviously an ad. The guard goes up. When they read text on screen, they hear it **in their own voice** - the same way you're reading this sentence. They are not being sold to anymore. They are literally telling themselves about your product.

Psychologists call it self-referential processing: information that runs through your own mental voice gets trusted more and believed more, because it feels like it came from you.

And the format filters out non-buyers naturally. Someone who won't take two minutes to read your message probably wasn't going to buy. The people who stick around are actively participating - they are doing the work of reading, invested, not passive.

## How to write for it

You are writing for the inner voice, not for speaking out loud. That is the opposite register from the [spoken-voice guide](spoken_voice.md) - a spoken script wants contractions and mid-thought openers; text on screen wants short bursts that read like natural thought patterns.

Instead of:
> "Have you ever wondered why some people have endless energy all day?"

Write:
> Some people have energy all day.
> You crash at 3pm. Why?

Short bursts. Easy to scan. One thought per block. The reader's inner voice does the pacing, so give it clean units to say.

The rest of the craft still applies - the [structure](script_principles.md) (educate before the product), the [VoC bank](../../brands/_TEMPLATE/research/voice_of_customer.md) for the customer's words (which ship verbatim here - typed language on a typed surface), and the written-copy tells to avoid.

## Editing rules that make or break it

- **Pacing is everything.** 3-5 seconds per text block depending on length. Give people time to actually read - a text-on-screen winner is deliberately slower than a voiceover cut.
- **Music becomes 10x more important.** Without a voice carrying emotion, the music IS the emotional driver. Dark, moody tracks for the pain points; uplifting builds for the solution. The music guides how they read the words.
- **Footage only has to be relevant, not synced.** Simple product shots, lifestyle footage, even aesthetic stock can work if it matches the vibe - its job is to keep the eyes engaged while the brain processes the words.
- **Slightly blurred or darkened backgrounds** make the text pop and keep focus where you want it.
- **Font: clean, sans-serif, high contrast.** No fancy treatments or animations that slow down reading speed.

## When to reach for it

- The avatar shows they like reading (good hold rates on text-heavy content, an audience that saves and screenshots).
- The message needs education the viewer must process, not just feel - a mechanism, a hormone story, a "here's why this is happening to you."
- The market is loud with UGC and creator ads - the format itself is the pattern interrupt.

Test it against a voiceover of the same script when you're unsure; the two often win for different avatars of the same brand.
