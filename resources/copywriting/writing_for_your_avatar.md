# Writing for Your Avatar - Style Follows the Person

Who you're selling to impacts literally everything: how you talk to them, what you say, which product features you highlight, how the ad is edited, and how the video feels. The same product sold to four different avatars is four entirely different ads. This guide is how to calibrate the WRITING to the person - the avatar doc (`brands/<brand>/research/avatars.md`) tells you who they are; this tells you what to do about it.

## The grandma test

Before writing a word: imagine making this ad for your grandma, then for your 20-year-old gym buddy. Everything changes - vocabulary, pace, proof, humor, bluntness, music, format. If your script would read the same for both, you haven't chosen an avatar yet, you've written for "everyone," and ads for everyone are for no one. On today's platforms **the creative IS the targeting** - the ad's look and language tell the algorithm who to find.

## Calibrate the bluntness

Different avatars tolerate completely different registers - same product, different worlds:

- **Blunt call-out** works for a thick-skinned avatar: "Dude, your case is trash and you know it."
- **Never blame the sensitive avatar.** If you blame her, she scrolls. Postpartum moms rejected every high-energy hype ad in the market; the winner AGREED with them ("Here's why gaining weight postpartum is normal"), removed the guilt ("it has nothing to do with your willpower"), and educated gently. Anti-hype, radical honesty, no negativity.
- **Spec-talk for the expert avatar.** The videographer wants the technical truth stated plainly; dumbing it down loses him, hyping it up insults him.

The rule: match their emotional state, not your energy. Frustrated people need understanding before solutions. Skeptical people need proof before promises. Proud people need respect before correction.

## Write YOUful

Copy should be full of *yous* - talking straight to one person, about their life, not about the product. "Nobody cares about the product. They only care about what it does for them." The word "you" should be doing a lot of heavy lifting in every script. The bar to clear is the highest one there is: they see the ad and think **"this is literally me."** Sometimes you earn that by spelling out their problem better than they could; sometimes by simply showing someone living their dream state so they recognize themselves and want in (identification marketing - show the product and the avatar in the dream state, and Chloe says "yeah, this is me").

## Steal their language

You cannot fake the way a niche talks - you mine it:

- Pull the exact phrases from Reddit threads, reviews, your own ad comments, and the raw research layer (`research/raw/` - your ad comments are the market giving you feedback in real time).
- Note the niche terms and jargon, and USE them - insiders hear an insider; outsiders writing in are spotted instantly.
- When AI drafts for you, make it research the avatar's language first and read the threads yourself - that's where lines like "new mom stress keeps cortisol high" come from.
- Watch what they watch. Make the ad look like something they would watch anyway - format, music, faces, setting, all avatar-native. The avatar doc's **Their Feed** section holds this as a production directive (creator type, production level, setting, delivery, pacing, formats, reference links) - read it before choosing an execution format, and never average two avatars' feeds into one look.

## Match the awareness level

Where the avatar sits on the awareness ladder decides how the ad OPENS (the full framework is in the [BA summary](../breakthrough-advertising/breakthrough_advertising_summary.md)):

| They are... | You open with... |
|---|---|
| Unaware | A story or identification - no product, no claims (the Dog Friendly shelter story) |
| Problem-aware | Their problem, named in their words |
| Solution-aware | The desire/solution, then your mechanism (CarePod's stainless steel) |
| Product-aware | Your superiority - the point of difference |
| Most aware | The product and the offer |

One product, many avatars usually also means many awareness levels - a nicotine user and a mushroom-coffee drinker need different ads for the same pouch.

## Keep it readable

Grade 6 or below on Hemingway (hemingwayapp.com), whoever the avatar is. Smart people happily read simple language; nobody reads complicated language in a feed. Sentence rhythm is avatar work too: staccato and punchy for momentum, softer and slower for sensitive audiences, plain and adjective-free when sincerity is the play.

## The checklist before you draft

1. Which avatar, by name, from the avatar doc?
2. What's their emotional state right now (frustrated / skeptical / hopeful / desperate)?
3. What have they already tried? (So you never pitch what already failed them.)
4. What language do they use for the problem? (Verbatim, from research.)
5. What awareness level - and so what does the opening look like?
6. How blunt can you be? What would make them scroll instantly?
7. What would they watch anyway - and does this ad look like that?
