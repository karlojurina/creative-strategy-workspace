# Script Principles - How We Write Copy

The working rules for writing and assembling ad copy. The [MANIFESTO](../../MANIFESTO.md) carries the mindset (copy is assembled, YOUful, see through the words); this doc carries the craft moves. Every one of these was earned on real ads - the [winning-ads case studies](../winning-ads/README.md) show them in action.

## The order of operations

1. **Research first, always.** You cannot write for someone you don't understand. Read the brand's research docs before a single line. If the AI will help draft, make IT research first too - send it to Reddit for how the avatar actually talks, and read the source threads yourself. Warmed up beats cold-prompted every time.
2. **Write your diagnosis in your own words before prompting.** What are you trying to do? Why this angle? What did the last ad teach you? If you can't write that paragraph, you're not ready to draft.
3. **Draft with full context.** Old scripts, learnings, the mechanism to feature, the execution format - the more tactical context in, the better the draft out. A bare "write me a script" gets you slop.
4. **Take it OUT of the chat and assemble it.** Into the brief doc, whole thing visible at once. Read it line by line in the customer's shoes. Fix with specific feedback ("the product enters too early because..."), not "make it better."
5. **Attach a strategic breakdown to every script.** What every segment is doing psychologically (the hook's job, the education block, the redefinition, the bridge). Your editor cuts better when they know what each beat is for - and YOU can't repeat a winner you can't explain.

## The structure of a persuasive script

Not a template - a sequence of jobs. Every line earns the next one.

1. **The hook opens a loop.** New information, a contradiction, or agreeing with them against the whole market's grain ("Here's why gaining weight postpartum is normal", "Everything you know about functional mushrooms is based on a biological lie"). The viewer's head now holds an open question. Curiosity first - names and products later.
2. **Meet them where they are.** Show them you understand their actual situation, in their language, fast. Remove the guilt if they carry any ("it has nothing to do with your willpower").
3. **Educate the problem.** This is the block most scripts skip - and it is the #1 script killer. Explain WHY their problem happens, simply, one idea at a time. Give the villain a name (a hormone, an industry secret, a broken delivery method). If they don't believe the problem story, nothing after it matters.
4. **Make it bigger.** Dig the knife: how long it lasts, what it costs them, why the usual fixes fail.
5. **Introduce the solution CATEGORY before the product.** "What if the problem isn't the mushrooms, but how they're delivered?" - the new thought, backed with borrowed proof (science, doctors, an undeniable analogy: "it's why nitroglycerin is placed under the tongue, not swallowed").
6. **Bridge to the product as the inevitable conclusion.** Best case: an exclusivity bridge - "the only [category] that contains [mechanism]." By now buying should feel like their own logical next thought, not your pitch.
7. **Visualize the desire.** Put them in the after: what tomorrow looks like with this working.
8. **Proof, then an honest CTA.** Close every loop you opened. Don't over-promise - "real results take time" outsold hype for a sensitive avatar.

**The one-line law behind all of it:** the more you educate people before introducing the product, the more ready they are to buy. Product-too-early is the most common way good angles die.

## Rules of thumb

- **Hemingway grade 6 or below** (hemingwayapp.com). If it grades higher, it's too complicated, and confusion = scroll. Aim lower.
- **Every line has a purpose.** If you can't say what job a line does, cut it.
- **Two appeal modes, pick deliberately:** logical (yes-stacking - small agreements that make the product make sense) vs emotional (feel first, logic follows). And two directions: bright side (paint the ideal life) vs dark side (paint the problem). Every product can be sold all four ways - choose based on the avatar, not habit.
- **Match tone to the avatar's sensitivity.** Some audiences (moms, health struggles) reject negativity and hype outright - agree with them, educate gently. Others take blunt.
- **Text-on-screen is a weapon.** Viewers read it with their inner voice - they sell the product to themselves. Slow the pace so they can actually process. Test it whenever an avatar shows they like reading.
- **Look organic, be tactical.** The best ads read like a person sharing, not a brand selling - while every sentence underneath is doing a persuasion job.
- **Emphasis is copy too.** Mark the lines the edit should hit (music out, words alone on screen) so the viewer FEELS the beat. Write the emphasis into the brief.
- **Hooks: test 3, expect one to teach you something.** Write hook variations against the same body (see `../prompts/hook-ideas.md`), and when one wins, write down WHY.

## The bar

Read the finished script one last time as the customer - a stranger, mid-scroll, zero patience. At every line ask: would I keep reading? Do I believe this yet? Was there a question in my head this line just answered? If the answer wobbles anywhere, the assembly isn't done.

Then read it OUT LOUD. Structure can be perfect and the script still dies if it sounds written instead of spoken - the read-aloud test and the written-copy tells live in [spoken_voice.md](spoken_voice.md), and every video script passes them before it ships.
