# Retention - Holding Them After the Hook

The hook buys you 3 seconds. Everything after is the hold, and the hold is where the selling happens. This guide is how to keep a viewer watching, line by line, until the CTA - and why most ads lose them in the first ten seconds after a great hook.

## The law: every line earns the next one

Copy is not paragraphs - it is **a series of headlines.** The headline's only job is to get the first line read; the first line's only job is to get the second read; the chain runs the entire ad. The moment one line fails to earn the next, the thumb moves. So read your script line by line asking one question: *after this line, why would they keep watching?*

## The silent dialogue

The viewer is talking back to your ad the whole time, silently:

- *"Tell me more."*
- *"Oh yeah? Who says so?"*
- *"How does it work?"*
- *"Okay, but what about me?"*

Great copy anticipates each demand and answers it at exactly the moment it forms. This is why you read the finished script in the customer's shoes: at every line, name the question currently in their head. If the line answers a question they don't have yet (product-too-early is the classic), there's your drop-off. If it repeats an answer they already accepted, there's your drag.

## Open loops - and close them

The hook opened a loop. The body can open more: an incomplete statement, a raised question with the answer implied later, a "but here's what changes everything." Loops are the engine of watch time - but **every loop you open must close before the CTA.** Viewers who finish the ad unsatisfied instead of resolved do not buy; unclosed loops are one of the standard reasons an ad spends but misses KPI.

Momentum phrases keep the pull going between loops: "Here's how." "And that's only the beginning." "For example -" Small invitations to read on, placed at the joints of the script.

## The bridge: seconds 4-10

The most common structural failure is not the hook and not the pitch - it's the bridge between them. The hook stops the viewer with curiosity; the body must pick up EXACTLY that thread, not start a new one. When an ad hits KPI but can't get spend, a broken hook-to-body bridge is one of the three usual causes. Watch the transition the way a stranger would: does second 5 pay off what second 2 promised?

## Educate to hold

The counterintuitive retention move: teach them something. When the viewer is *getting value* - learning why their problem happens, what a hormone does, why 90% of a supplement never reaches their brain - watching the ad is worth their time on its own, and hold rates climb. Education is not a detour from selling; done right it IS the selling, because the more they understand before the product appears, the more ready they are to buy. ## Pacing and emphasis

- **Give them time to process.** A slower edit can out-hold a fast one when the ideas need absorbing - the Metaglow text-on-screen winner was deliberately slower than the usual house pace.
- **Text-on-screen makes them participants.** Viewers read it with their inner voice - they sell the product to themselves. Test it wherever the avatar shows they like reading.
- **Write the emphasis beats into the brief.** Mark the lines where the music stops and the words stand alone on screen ("you are being played a fool") - a felt beat resets attention mid-ad.
- **Vary the texture.** When copy runs too long in one mode (promise after promise, or fact after fact), it goes dead. Sense the satiation point and shift: from promise to proof, from problem to story, from education to demonstration.

## How long should the ad be?

As long as it takes to (1) build the desire to full strength, (2) create identification, and (3) overcome objections and build belief - no more, no less. A two-minute ad that educates can beat a snappy 40-second one (Metaglow's winner was 2x the length of the ad it replaced). Length is not the risk; unearned lines are.

## Diagnosing retention problems

Soft metrics tell you where the leak is: hook rate fine but hold rate poor = the bridge or the body; watch-time cliff at one timestamp = find the line at that second and ask which question it failed to answer. Full diagnostic flow: [learnings-playbook.md](../learnings-playbook.md).
