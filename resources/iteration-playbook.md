# Iteration Playbook

Iteration is the cheapest of the three concept types - the market already voted on the core. You are not guessing anymore; you are refining a proven signal.

## First, identify what you're working with

Before touching anything, pull up the ad in Meta and work through this. It pairs with the learnings playbook - the verdict, comments, and soft metrics come from there.

1. **Identify the outcome** (the four verdicts) and note the ad's total spend AND the campaign's total spend in the same period.
2. **Identify the ad type** - 💡 Ideation (idea came from research), 🎭 Imitation (from another brand's ad), 🔄 Iteration (from an existing ad).
3. **Write out every hook VERBATIM.** Do not summarize. If the ad received significant spend, also note hook rate and hold rate. What counts as significant depends on the account - use judgment relative to the campaign (recommendation: $1k in spend minimum after 7 days).
4. **Name the components, explicitly:**
   - What is the **angle** (the reason someone will buy)?
   - What is the **mechanism**?
   - What is the **authority** building trust?
   - Who is the **avatar**?
   - What is the **desire**?
   - What is the **awareness level**?
   - What is the **emotion** (valence + intensity)?
   - What are we doing that's **different** (positioning)?
5. **Verify planned vs executed.** Check the concept card in the growth guide against what is actually IN the ad. Does the copy match the awareness level you planned? Does it communicate to the avatar you picked? Is the mechanism actually communicated? Note every difference - a "failed angle" is often just an unexecuted plan.
6. **Write your hypothesis.** Why do you think this ad performed the way it did? What specifically worked or didn't? Consider what is working in your other ads and winning ads in general to avoid making false learnings. Be specific.
7. **Link the final ad file** on the concept card so you can always find it later.

Then go to the section for your verdict below.

## ❌ Loser

*The problem: the ad isn't getting spend and isn't hitting KPI. Nobody cared - and most of the time that means the hook simply did not stop people.*

**Most common reasons ads are losers:**

1. The hook did not grab attention (usually a hook rate under 30%)
2. The overall angle was just off (too niche, too broad, nobody cares)
3. Unaware ad - it was a wildcard, a really big swing that just missed
4. Problem/solution aware - the hook was not relatable or the problem wasn't painful enough
5. Product/most aware - the offer is just not competitive, the differentiation is not clear

**What we iterate on losers:** 💡 Ideation - we may try the idea 2-3 more times, but only with evidence for the new version. 🔄 Iteration - identify what went wrong in execution. **We do NOT iterate losing 🎭 imitation ads.**

**The loser fixes:**

| Fix | What it means |
|-----|---------------|
| **Hook Issue #1 - engagement** | Make the hook more engaging so people actually stop their scroll and Meta can push it out to more people. |
| **Hook Issue #2 - audience signal** | The visuals and messaging don't tell Meta's systems WHO to serve the ad to. A man looking in a mirror could be about anything; a needle plus a jacked-dude drawing is clearly about testosterone. |
| **TAM Issue** | The angle or messaging/labels are too niche. The ad resonates with some, but the group is too narrow (men ON TRT vs men CONSIDERING TRT). |
| **Broad Issue** | The opposite: too broad AND the copy isn't strong enough to capture anyone. The ad resonates with no one. |
| **Valence/Intensity check** | Shift the emotional tone. Positive → try fear/guilt. Negative → try aspirational. Intense → dial down. Mild → crank up. |
| **Empty Claims in a Sophisticated Market** | You're making claims with zero proof, zero facts, zero anything - in a market that has heard it all. |
| **Bridge Issue** | High hook rate, low hold rate = the bridge between hook and hold is bad or awkward. An opinion-based bridge loses people; a fact-based bridge holds them. |
| **Offer Issue** | They don't see the value (price-to-value is off for this audience). Study the price objections and weave the counters in. |

## 🎯 KPI Winner

*The problem: converting, but not getting breakthrough levels of spend.*

Two flavors - figure out which one yours is:

1. **A loser that got lucky.** Very low spend % with a few purchases usually means lucky attribution or very warm buyers. Treat it exactly like a loser (the fixes above).
2. **A deliberate bottom-of-funnel / offer / retargeting ad.** These are not supposed to scale - they hold up the account's overall ROAS and convert people your colder prospecting ads warmed up. If that was the plan, the ad is doing its job. Leave it.

Also common: the angle resonates with a small sector but is simply not scalable (too niche → TAM fix).

**What we iterate:** same as losers. We rarely iterate KPI-winning 🎭 imitation ads.

## 💸 Spend Winner

*The problem: breakthrough levels of spend, but the KPI is below the account average and the account isn't scaling.*

A spend winner means you did something right - the algorithm sees the ad is scalable and pushes it toward colder and colder audiences. But not all spend winners are equal: one does 0.3x ROAS, another does 1.8x against a 2.0x target. Either way, the ad is NOT scaling your account.

**Diagnose first.** ROAS is made of CPM, CPC, CVR, and AOV - compare each against the account and find which one is lacking. Then dig deeper: hook rate, hold rate, hook-to-hold %, average play time, frequency, % of new visitors; use the breakdown tool for age, gender, placement, platform.

**What it usually is:**

- Lacking **trust (Belief)** → fix by adding more authority
- Lacking **Urgency** → fix by emphasizing the problem with waiting, why act TODAY
- Lacking **Stakes** → fix by escalating and raising the stakes in the ad
- A **Funnel/Congruence issue** → the hook sets an expectation the landing page doesn't match; fix the LP
- The hook is **misleading people** → disconnect between what people expect and what's on the page
- **Open loops you never closed** → viewers were intrigued but felt unsatisfied at the end

**The trap - "hold iteration mode."** Don't assume "my hook works, now I'll test different holds." The hook can be the ROOT CAUSE of the conversion problem: it stops people, but sets them up in a way that makes them impossible to convince. Attention alone is not the goal. Attention that BUYS is.

**What we iterate:** 💡 Ideation that gets spend - always try the idea 3-5 more times (with a real direction for better execution, not blind repeats). 🎭 Imitation working well - try the idea 2-3 more times to test execution. 🔄 Iteration - fix the specific leak.

Then pull moves from the menu below - Bridge Remake, Overcoming Objections, and the awareness levels test are the usual first picks here.

## 🏆 Breakthrough

The angle is proven. Immediately create iteration tasks from the menu below - widen it before it fatigues.

**If your breakthrough ads keep dying fast (the TIME issue), check:**

1. The avatar needs expanding (self identity + valence + intensity) - increase TAM
2. It went too bottom-of-funnel and just picked up the scraps
3. Try a higher awareness level (problem aware / unaware)
4. You're not resonating on an emotional level - to actually scale, you have to talk to people's emotions
5. People realize over time it's bullshit (fake AI native story ads)
6. A competitor launched a better offer/creative that outscales you
7. Seasonal demand shifted (humidifiers in July)
8. Seasonal messaging/visuals fatigued (winter background running in summer)

**What holds breakthrough ads back structurally:** a TAM ceiling, being **easily replicable** (native/low-effort ads with no clear differentiation that competitors can copy overnight), or the product itself lacking **differentiation/positioning**.

## The rules

- **One move per variation.** Keep the winning core untouched - change everything and you learn nothing.
- **Don't run the whole menu at once.** Pick the moves your hypothesis points at, high priority first, each as its own variation.
- **Keep the lineage.** Every iteration card sets `Iterates:` to the parent card's id. In the ad account, put the iteration in the name too (pattern: `ITER#[N]_BATCH#[ORIG]` right after the concept name) so you can always trace a variation back to its parent.

## The menu: winner iterations

### Hook & script iterations

| # | Move | What it is | Priority |
|---|------|-----------|----------|
| 1 | **Winning Hook Remake** | Remake with the winning hook. Keep what worked, fix what was sloppy. | 🔴 High |
| 2 | **Bridge Remake** | Optimize the bridge to establish more trust, create more urgency, and keep the audience hooked in between the hook and the hold. | 🔴 High |
| 3 | **Extended Problem Section** | Create a version with a longer problem/agitation section. Go deeper on the pain, add more specific pain points, make the viewer feel it more before presenting the solution. | 🔴 High |
| 4 | **Awareness Levels Test** | Identify the ad's current awareness level and shift it up (sometimes down) the scale. Example ladder for a cat toy: Product aware "The most addicting cat toy" → Solution aware "I tried everything to keep my cat busy" → Problem aware "I didn't realize my cat being bored could take years off its life..." → Unaware "Who has a better life... cats or dogs?" | 🟡 Medium |
| 5 | **In Media Res** | Start the ad with the unique mechanism (show don't tell). Get straight to the point in the first 5 seconds - start with the ideal outcome, then start explaining how we got there. | 🟡 Medium |
| 6 | **Overcoming Objections** | Pull real objections from comments under the ad. Create a new variation that naturally overcomes those objections within the script - don't make it obvious, just weave them in. | 🔴 High |
| 7 | **Comment Reply Hook** | Use a real comment or objection as the hook (screenshot of the comment), then play the winning ad as the "reply." | 🔴 High |
| 8 | **Dumber Version** | Convert the script to natural spoken language. Add filler words, pauses, casual phrasing. Make the script way dumber - it should feel too simple. | 🟡 Medium |
| 9 | **Add Soft CTAs** | Add soft CTAs throughout the ad. | 🟡 Medium |
| 10 | **Negative Framing** | Flip the entire ad to argue AGAINST the product sarcastically - "Honestly, don't buy this. Here's why..." then disprove each point. | 🟡 Medium |
| 11 | **Script as Framework - New Sub-Avatars** | Keep the winning script but rewrite/adjust for a completely different customer avatar (different age, life stage, use case). | 🟡 Medium |

### Format & style iterations

| # | Move | What it is | Priority |
|---|------|-----------|----------|
| 12 | **VO ↔ Text-on-Screen** | If the original had voiceover, test a text-on-screen only version. If it was text-on-screen, test a voiceover version. | 🔴 High |
| 13 | **Organic/Ugly Edit** | Re-edit the ad with ugly, simple editing like a normal person would make. Raw, native, unpolished. | 🔴 High |
| 14 | **VSL Expansion** | Expand the core idea into a longer-form VSL style ad. Go deeper on education, mechanism, and proof. | 🔴 High |
| 15 | **AI Animated Ingredients/Mechanisms** | Personify specific ingredients or parts of the mechanism to communicate the same angle. Turn the ingredient into the hero. | 🟡 Medium |
| 16 | **AI Animated Solutions** | Personify the OTHER solutions in your market. Villainize the alternatives, show yours as the hero. | 🟡 Medium |
| 17 | **AI Animated Body Parts / Hormones** | Personify the body parts or hormones involved. Have them talk to communicate the angle, or villainize the hormone causing the problem. | 🟡 Medium |
| 18 | **AI Singing Version** | Take the winning angle and script and turn it into a song with AI animation. | 🟡 Medium |
| 19 | **AI Animated Story (No Human)** | Turn the winning ad moments into an AI ad with a NON-human character telling the story (Pixar, crochet, claymation). | 🟡 Medium |
| 20 | **AI Animated Story (Human)** | Same, with a human character telling the story. | 🟡 Medium |
| 21 | **AI UGC Narration** | Use an AI person narrating on top of a video to tell the story. | 🟡 Medium |
| 22 | **Podcast Style** | Reformat the winning script into a podcast-style conversation between two people discussing the topic. | 🟡 Medium |
| 23 | **Street Interview Style** | Conduct (or hire out) street interviews following the same winning components. | 🟡 Medium |
| 24 | **New Location/Setting/Vibe** | Same script and concept, different visual environment. New room, outdoor vs. indoor, different backdrop. | 🟡 Medium |

### Scaling & stacking iterations

| # | Move | What it is | Priority |
|---|------|-----------|----------|
| 25 | **Stack Winners Mega-Ad** | Combine 3-4 winning video clips/hooks into one longer compilation ad. | 🟡 Medium |
| 26 | **UGC Creators** | Get 2-3 different creators to execute the same winning concept (and script, if possible) in their own style. Vary age, gender, energy. | 🔴 High |
| 27 | **Founder/Authority Delivery** | Have the brand founder or an authority figure (vet, doctor, expert) deliver the winning script. | 🟡 Medium |

### Emotional testing

| # | Move | What it is | Priority |
|---|------|-----------|----------|
| 28 | **Valence/Intensity Test** | Shift the emotional tone of the winning ad. If positive, try fear/guilt. If negative, try aspirational. If intense, dial it down. If mild, crank it up. | 🔴 High |
| 29 | **Self-Discrepancy Test** | Create variations for the different versions of self: ideal self, ought self, actual self. | 🟡 Medium |

### Cross-format iterations

| # | Move | What it is | Priority |
|---|------|-----------|----------|
| 30 | **Static Ad Extraction** | Pull the single strongest moment/claim from the video and turn it into a static ad. | 🔴 High |
| 31 | **First Frame Static Ad** | Take the first frame of the ad, come up with copy based on the script, and turn that into a static ad. | 🟡 Medium |
| 32 | **Native Ad** | Create a static ad with primary copy that mirrors the winning video script. Feels like organic content, not an ad. | 🟡 Medium |
| 33 | **Static Ad + Congruent LP** | Create a static version of the winning concept AND build a landing page that matches the specific angle/messaging. | 🔴 High |

## Example: the Identify step done right

A breakthrough ad for Uproot Clean (washing machine enzyme tablets), identified:

- **Desire (why-chain it down):** "I want a clean washing machine" → why? → "it's making my clothes smell bad" → why do you care? → "the whole point of cleaning clothes is for them to smell good" → why? → "if they smell bad, that could be a health hazard" → **health core desire**.
- **Avatar:** pet owner with a washing machine that doesn't smell good. Experiences: clothes musty for months, house smells like damp fur. Behaviors: tried different detergents, scrubbed the rubber with bleach, hot cycles with vinegar, extra rinse cycles. Emotions: frustrated, grossed out.
- **Angle:** will fix the bad smell coming from your washing machine.
- **Mechanism:** enzyme tablet - and it gets explained, not just named.
- **Authority:** the 30-year washing machine Grandpa, plus unaware-level language later in the video (explaining the deep-down details).
- **Awareness:** solution aware opening, with unaware sections later in the video.
- **Emotion:** low valence, low intensity.

That level of specificity is what makes the iteration moves obvious instead of random.

## What iteration is NOT

- A rescue mission for a dead angle (and never a re-run of a losing imitation ad)
- A new idea wearing the old card's id (new desire + new avatar + new mechanism = Ideation)
- Infinite - two loser iterations in a row means the angle is tapped
