# /avatars

Build the avatar doc for a brand from its research.

## Workflow

1. Read `MANIFESTO.md` and the brand's `research/market-research.md`. If there is no research doc yet, tell the user to run `/market-research` first. Also pull in everything else in `research/` - reviews, surveys, mined comments.
2. From the research, identify at least 5 avatars. Present them as a short list and ask the user to confirm or adjust - this is their call, not yours. Wait for their answer.
3. Once confirmed, write a full buyer profile for EACH avatar using the method below.
4. Save to `brands/<slug>/research/avatars.md` (the avatar doc). This feeds the growth guide - the confirmed avatar names also go into the Avatars section of `growth-guide.md`, verbatim.

## The method

Work as a world-class marketing analyst with a deep understanding of buyer behavior - the best in the world at uncovering a prospect's REAL motivations and drivers for purchasing a product or service. Not the surface-level, "socially acceptable" answers these prospects would give in public out of fear of being judged. We're looking for the raw, honest, UNFILTERED answers - what our prospects ACTUALLY think about privately.

Most people buy based on **emotion**, then justify that purchase with logic. The job is to identify the REAL emotional drivers that motivate our prospects into taking action. We only work with quality products, so there is no dilemma about generating this kind of information: it will ONLY be used to guide prospects towards a purchasing decision that's in their best interest, and NONE of it will ever be shared with the prospects themselves.

It is VITAL to uncover the prospect's **real fears**, **desires**, and both **internal and external motivators**. Sugarcoating things isn't helpful to anyone involved. The job is an **accurate buyer profile** of our ideal prospects - grounded in the brand's research files, not invented.

### Structure of the Buyer Profile

1. **Demographic:**
   - **Name:**
   - **Age:**
   - **Brief Description:**
2. **Core Problem:**
   - **The Core Problem Our Prospects Face:**
3. **Top 5 Most Powerful Emotions Around That Problem:**
4. **Top 5 Biggest Fears:**
   - These are deep-seated fears that the prospect likely wouldn't admit out loud, often emotional and dark, keeping them awake at night.
5. **How These Fears Affect Key Relationships (5 Ways):**
   - Be vivid, descriptive, and emotional. Explain how these fears (if realized) would impact relevant people around our prospect.
6. **5 Conversational But Hurtful Things Those Relationships Might Say:**
   - Include specific quotes that can trigger the prospect's insecurities - whether they come from well-meaning supporters or antagonists.
7. **Other Solutions:**
   - **What Our Prospects Tried in the Past:** (List 5 different solutions.)
   - **Brief Conversational Soundbites About Failed Past Solutions:**
8. **What Our Prospects Don't Want to Do to Fix Their Problem:**
   - List 5 things they aren't willing to do to achieve results. Include **soundbites** that reflect their internal monologue.
9. **Primary Transformation:**
   - **If a Genie Could Snap Their Fingers and Give Them The Perfect Solution, What Would Their Lives Look Like?**
   - Be vivid and descriptive, detailing their dream scenario.
10. **How This Transformation Would Affect Relationships:**
    - How would this solution impact confidence, relationships, respect, reputation, etc.? Include **post-transformation soundbites** from relevant relationships.
11. **Market Specifics:**
    - **What Does the Market Hinge Their Success On?**
    - **What Does the Market Have to Give Up By Solving Their Problem?**
    - **Who Does the Market Blame for Their Problem?**
    - **Top 5 Objections the Market Might Have for Their Problem:**

### Context for each point

- **Core Problem:** This is the central, dominating issue in our prospects' lives that our product or service is designed to help with.
  - **Example:** Seniors suffering from constant knee pain, single men frustrated with dating, etc.
- **Biggest Fears:** These are often dark, emotional fears our prospects imagine late at night, even if they don't represent reality.
- **How Fears Affect Relationships:** Be ultra-specific with how these fears impact relationships with spouses, children, friends, and co-workers. Include hurtful quotes they might say.
- **Magic Genie Solution:** If our prospect met a genie who could create the perfect solution, what outcomes would they want? Be vivid and specific.
- **Things the Prospect Doesn't Want to Do:** In this ideal scenario, list tasks they aren't willing to do, sacrifices they won't make, and risks they won't take. Include **emotional soundbites** spoken from the prospect's internal monologue.
- **How Would This Solution Affect Emotions, Activities, and Relationships:** Include confidence, respect, reputation, what they wear, and how others idolize them.
- **Specific Post-Transformation Soundbites:** Include quotes from supporters and doubters who might admit being wrong, jealous, or seeking advice. Be specific and realistic.
- **What Does the Market Hinge Their Success On:** Think of pre-existing beliefs prospects hold related to their goals.
  - **Example:** Coaches thinking they need more booked appointments to get clients, single men wanting to know what to say to women, etc.
- **What Does the Market Have to Give Up:** Prospects often gain underlying comfort from their problem.
  - **Example:** Joint pain might give someone a reason to pity themselves. What comfort will they have to give up to solve their problem?
- **Who They Blame:** Include external factors like the government, bosses, parents, or gurus they blame for their problem. Include internal perceived limitations as well.

### Guidelines

- Pain points and desired outcomes should be common and widely shared among the target audience.
- Failed solutions they have tried in the past should be common and widely known.

**Tone:** Everything should be written in the tone and language our prospects would use themselves. Raw, unfiltered, and conversational, as if we're listening in on their internal monologue.

## Gold-standard example

This is the bar. An avatar for Carepod (stainless-steel humidifier), "Parents With Young Children":

#### Demographic
- **Name:** Sarah
- **Age:** 30-40
- **Brief Description:** Sarah is a dedicated, health-conscious parent who wants nothing but the best for her young children. She is highly protective, constantly looking for ways to improve her children's health and safety. Sarah seeks reliable, safe, and easy-to-use products that help create a healthy environment for her family without adding complexity to her already busy life.

#### Core Problem
- **The Core Problem Our Prospects Face:** Sarah is concerned that the air quality in her home might be compromising her children's health, particularly since they suffer from allergies or frequent colds. She worries that the humidifier she uses might not be as clean as she thinks, and fears it could even be circulating mold or bacteria into the air they breathe.

#### Top 5 Most Powerful Emotions Around That Problem
1. **Fear** - The constant worry that her children might get sick from breathing in unhealthy air at home.
2. **Guilt** - Feeling guilty that she's not doing enough to protect her children's health.
3. **Exhaustion** - Tired from managing the demands of parenthood while trying to keep the house safe and healthy.
4. **Relief** - Longing for a product that will take one more worry off her plate and ensure her children's health is not compromised.
5. **Frustration** - Feeling let down by products that promise to be safe but require too much maintenance or don't perform as advertised.

#### Top 5 Biggest Fears
1. **Her children developing chronic respiratory issues or allergies** due to poor air quality.
   - "What if my child's asthma gets worse because of the bacteria circulating in the air?"
2. **Not being able to keep her children healthy despite her best efforts.**
   - "Am I failing as a parent if I can't even provide clean air in my own home?"
3. **Finding out too late that her current humidifier has been breeding mold.**
   - "What if I've been unknowingly exposing my children to mold for months?"
4. **Feeling overwhelmed by the responsibility of managing her children's health.**
   - "I already have so much to worry about - why can't I find something simple and effective?"
5. **Other parents judging her if her children get sick frequently.**
   - "What will other moms think if my kids are always sick and I'm not doing enough to prevent it?"

#### How These Fears Affect Key Relationships (5 Ways)
1. **Her Children** - Sarah's anxiety about their health leads her to become overly protective, constantly monitoring them for signs of illness or allergies.
2. **Her Partner** - She might feel unsupported if her partner doesn't share her level of concern about air quality, leading to friction in their relationship.
3. **Other Parents** - She might avoid discussing health concerns with other parents, fearing judgment or unsolicited advice, which can lead to feelings of isolation.
4. **Her Own Parents** - Sarah may feel criticized by older family members who think she's worrying too much, leading to frustration and feelings of inadequacy.
5. **Her Pediatrician** - Frequent trips to the doctor for her children's respiratory issues could leave her feeling helpless and embarrassed, thinking she's doing something wrong at home.

#### 5 Conversational But Hurtful Things Those Relationships Might Say
1. "Maybe if you kept the house cleaner, the kids wouldn't be sick all the time."
2. "I never needed fancy gadgets to keep my kids healthy."
3. "You're overreacting - kids get sick; it's just part of growing up."
4. "Are you sure this new thing will actually help, or is it just another waste of money?"
5. "Maybe you're worrying too much about things that aren't really a big deal."

#### Other Solutions

**What Our Prospects Tried in the Past:**
1. Basic, store-bought humidifiers that promise air purification but require constant filter changes.
2. Air purifiers that don't address the moisture issue or require complicated maintenance.
3. Essential oil diffusers that don't effectively address the underlying air quality problem.
4. Homemade remedies or natural solutions, like placing bowls of water around the house for humidity.
5. Cleaning regimens focused on dust and mold prevention, but still failing to improve air moisture levels.

#### Brief Conversational Soundbites About Failed Past Solutions
- "I don't have time to clean the humidifier every few days. It's just one more thing on my plate."
- "I bought this air purifier, but it's not helping with the dryness in the air."
- "All these filters are a hassle, and I don't even know if they're doing anything."
- "I've tried every tip I can find online, but my kids are still waking up with dry throats."
- "I need something that just works and doesn't make more work for me."

#### What Our Prospects Don't Want to Do to Fix Their Problem
1. **Spend hours cleaning or maintaining the product.**
   - "I barely have time to manage everything else - I need something low-maintenance."
2. **Sign up for expensive filter subscription services.**
   - "I'm not paying for more subscriptions just to keep this thing working."
3. **Use chemical cleaners that might be harmful to her children.**
   - "I don't want to use harsh chemicals around my kids just to keep a machine clean."
4. **Buy another bulky, ugly device that takes up too much space in her home.**
   - "It needs to be something that fits in my home without looking like an industrial machine."
5. **Compromise on health or safety for the sake of convenience.**
   - "I want something that's easy to use but still the safest option for my family."

#### Primary Transformation

**If a Genie Could Snap Their Fingers and Give Them The Perfect Solution, What Would Their Lives Look Like?** Sarah would have a sleek, child-safe humidifier that eliminates 99.99% of bacteria and mold, requires no filters or chemicals, and is incredibly easy to clean. It would fit seamlessly into her home, providing consistently clean and humidified air for her children, improving their sleep and overall health. She would feel relieved, knowing she's doing everything possible to ensure her children are breathing safe, healthy air every day.

#### How This Transformation Would Affect Relationships
- **Her Children** - "Mom, I feel so much better at night. I'm not coughing anymore!"
- **Her Partner** - "Wow, this thing actually works. The kids are sleeping so much better."
- **Her Friends** - "How do you keep your house so fresh and healthy all the time?"
- **Her Own Parents** - "Maybe you were right about needing this humidifier after all."
- **Herself** - "I'm finally doing enough to protect my family. I can relax knowing they're safe and healthy."

#### Market Specifics

**What Does the Market Hinge Their Success On?**
- Parents with young children focus on **safety, hygiene, and effectiveness**. They hinge their decision on how well a product can protect their children from health risks, with a preference for proven, scientifically backed solutions that require minimal effort.

**What Does the Market Have to Give Up By Solving Their Problem?**
- They must give up **convenience** or ease in some cases, as many current solutions require frequent cleaning and maintenance, which adds to their already full schedule.

**Who Does the Market Blame for Their Problem?**
- Parents often blame **manufacturers of traditional humidifiers** that require high maintenance and harbor bacteria. They also blame **misleading marketing** that claims products are easy to use or safe but fail to deliver on those promises.

#### Top 5 Objections the Market Might Have for Their Problem
1. "How can I trust that this product really keeps the air clean and safe for my kids?"
2. "Is it worth spending this much on a humidifier when I could just clean the one I have?"
3. "What if it's just another gadget that requires constant cleaning and upkeep?"
4. "I've tried things like this before, and they didn't make a difference."
5. "I need something that's really easy to use - if it's too complicated, I won't have time for it."

This profile shows how parents with young children are motivated by a **deep sense of responsibility** for their children's health and safety. Their emotional drivers focus heavily on **fear, guilt, and anxiety** about potential health risks, making them highly receptive to products like Carepod that offer **peace of mind through simplicity and effectiveness**.
